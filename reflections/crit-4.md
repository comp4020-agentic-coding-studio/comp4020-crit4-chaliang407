## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The breakthrough was realizing "it works" wasn't actually the bar for this
project. The first version of Orbit technically satisfied the brief — drag to
play, release to stop — but playing it made that stop mattering. Sometimes it
felt monotonous even though every sound was technically correct, or the
screen looked empty, or controls I'd just added turned out to be invisible
against the background. None of that shows up by running a check or reading a
diff — only by sitting there dragging my mouse around and noticing I was
bored, or that something felt missing. Manual play-testing wasn't a
nice-to-have step at the end; it was the thing telling me what to build next.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

Working with an agent didn't remove the need for judgement — it made it the
whole job. The code was rarely the hard part; noticing "this feels empty" and
turning that into one specific, bounded change was. I want to get better at
that translation, and at the restraint alongside it: every problem I noticed
while playing could have become an excuse to add more features, and instead I
had to keep asking what actually needed to change and what should stay
untouched. I don't think this made me a different developer. It made me trust
my own reaction to something as real evidence, worth acting on.
